Approach 2 question

Does an AI opportunity scanner produce novel ideas?

  1. 1

    Asked directly, no. The zero-shot scanner's 30 ideas were useful and feasible, but 26 already existed as patents, products or regulator proposals. For example, RBI's April 2026 cancellable 1-hour transfer delay already covers its "dispute escrow window".

  2. 2

    Grounded in live demand, yes. The scanner fed CFPB complaint trends, new rules and news, scored for patent whitespace, produced the most surviving and most commercially useful inventions: 4 of 16.

  3. 3

    Cross-pollination produced the most surprising ideas, and the most collisions with academia. The deep check removed 4 of its 5 early winners because papers from 2020 to 2026 had already described them.

  4. 4

    Embedding similarity alone gets novelty backwards. One-line ideas look novel because short text resembles nothing. Once text length was controlled, the three arms were almost identical.

  • Novelty needs a relevance gateWithout one, the most "novel" items were politics and celebrity news, because they are farthest from banking.
  • Compare within genreHeadlines look novel against a paper and patent library purely because of their format.
  • Noise looks like whitespaceNobody patents conference promotions. Filter notices, collapse syndicated stories and query whitespace with phrases.
  • Similarity bands need calibrationA patent's abstract and its own claim score 0.86 on median; different patents in the same class score up to 0.78.

Method notes: one run, 69 ideas. The same model generated and judged the ideas, so automatic similarity is reported alongside the judge's scores, and every surviving idea was checked with targeted searches. Non-US patent families are under-covered because Google Patents throttled after about 1,100 documents; run Espacenet and InPASS checks before filing. This is not legal advice.
